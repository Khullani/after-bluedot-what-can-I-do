# Facilitator's Wrap-Up Note — Template

> The closing note a facilitator sends to their cohort 1–2 days after the
> final session. Captures clarity scores, individual commitments, shared
> themes, recommended reading, and a follow-up mechanism. Hand the playbook
> over inside this note — not separately.

---

Hi everyone,

I wanted to send one final note to close out our [course name] course
— and to thank each of you for the seriousness, curiosity, and
generosity you brought to the cohort.

On our last day, I asked each of you two questions:

1. On a scale from 0–10, how clear are you on your next steps?
2. What would help move you closer to a 10 — not just intellectually,
   but in a way that drives action?

I'm sharing a synthesis here because I think the answers were
thoughtful, specific, and worth preserving — so you have a record for
yourselves, and so I can ask you about your progress over the coming
months.

## Individual reflections

> considering, what would help them commit, and the concrete next step
> they named on the last call. If somebody missed the final session,
> leave a placeholder and ask for their reflection.

**[Name] — [score]/10 clarity**
[2–3 sentences on their situation, the paths they're weighing, and
what would move them closer to a 10.]
*Next step:* [Concrete thing they committed to.]

**[Name] — [score]/10 clarity**
[Same shape.]
*Next step:* [Same.]

[...one per cohort member.]

**[Name we missed]**
We missed you on the last day, and hope you had safe travels. Please
share your reflections.

## Shared themes

> 4–6 themes you actually heard, not generic ones. The right tone is
> "what I noticed across the room" — observational, not directive.

1. **Everyone has direction, but not full conviction yet.** [1–2
   sentences expanding on what that looked like.]
2. **The next step is different for each person.** [Same.]
3. **The hardest part is not just learning more.** It's deciding:
   What am I willing to commit to? What question is actually mine
   to pursue? What form of action fits my skills, context, and
   obligations?
4. **AI governance is not one path.** [Examples from the cohort's
   own paths.]
5. **Everyone has ideas they are not writing about and publishing.**
   Everyone should be writing and publishing — even just on Substack
   — if for no other reason than to think clearly, and to take
   public positions.

## Maintaining hope and optimism

In this course I focused on the things that could go wrong so you can
prepare for them. But you also need a positive vision of the world
to work toward, and a way to keep that vision alive when the
material gets heavy.

A few of my favorite memoirs from people in genuinely difficult
circumstances who found a way:

- *The Gulag Archipelago* — Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn
- *Hope Against Hope* — Nadezhda Mandelstam
- *Persepolis* — Marjane Satrapi
- *Reading Lolita in Tehran* — Azar Nafisi
- *The Girl Who Smiled Beads* — Clemantine Wamariya
- *The Years of Extermination* — Saul Friedländer
- *Long Walk to Freedom* — Nelson Mandela
- *First They Killed My Father* — Loung Ung
- *The Unwomanly Face of War* — Svetlana Alexievich

Plus poetry as a genre, and science fiction as an operating manual.

The reason this matters for AI governance is that the field can
become abstract very quickly. These books pull you back toward
lived experience: fear, coercion, adaptation, courage, institutional
failure, propaganda, scarcity, and moral choice under pressure.

Please don't hesitate to reach out if I can be of any help.

[Your name]

## Notes on use

- **Send within 48 hours of the last session.** Memory of the closing
  call decays fast. The note works when the cohort can still hear
  their own voices in it.
- **Name people, not roles.** "[Name] — start a Substack tomorrow"
  lands. "Cohort members should consider publishing" does not.
- **Be willing to be a little ridiculous.** Tease people gently.
  Reference inside jokes from the cohort. Use emoji where they fit.
  A note that reads like a corporate email is one that gets
  archived; a note that reads like the facilitator who was actually
  in the room is one that gets reread.
- **Promise the follow-up explicitly.** "I'll be asking about each of
  these over the coming months" is what turns the commitments into
  commitments. Then actually do it.
- **Hand over the playbook inside this note**, not in a separate email.
  The wrap-up note is the warmth; the playbook is the structure. They
  work better together than apart.
