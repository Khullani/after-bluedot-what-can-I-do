# Public Debate — Moderator Research Prompt

Paste the text below into a capable research model or deep-research tool to
build a landscape briefing. Fill the `[BRACKETS]`, verify the citations it
returns, and distill the result into a one-page cue sheet for the event.

---

You are helping me prepare to moderate an Oxford-style public debate on the
motion: *"[MOTION]"*. The date is [DATE]; the debaters are [NAMES +
AFFILIATIONS]. I need to be fluent across the whole spectrum of serious views,
not advocate for any of them. Produce a structured briefing with these
sections:

1. **The core question** — what the motion actually asks, and the main ways
   "[KEY TERM]" can be interpreted (list the distinct versions).
2. **Who's who** — the debaters and their organizations: their known
   positions, best arguments, and likely pressure points. Note intra-side
   nuance (where teammates might disagree).
3. **The strongest case for** — steel-manned, with its best evidence.
4. **The strongest case against** — steel-manned, with its best evidence.
5. **The real cruxes** — the 3–5 disagreements that actually decide the
   question (for example, timelines, probability of loss of control,
   enforceability, and whether progress requires the frontier).
6. **The evidence base** — the most-cited data, reports, and studies each side
   leans on, with what they do and don't show.
7. **Alternatives to the binary** — the middle-ground and “third-way”
   positions.
8. **Commonly misstated facts** — claims that sound authoritative but are
   wrong or contested, so I can catch them live.
9. **A one-page night-of card** — the motion, the format, 8–10 balanced
   cross-examination questions (including “state the other side's strongest
   point”), and 5–6 facts at a glance.

Cite primary sources wherever possible. Link every source. Distinguish facts,
estimates, arguments, and your own inferences. Flag where the evidence is
genuinely uncertain. Keep me neutral throughout.

---

**After you get the briefing:** verify its factual claims and citations, then
cut it to a single page you can hold — the motion, your opening framing, the
cross-examination questions, and the facts you want at your fingertips. That
is what you actually use on the night.
