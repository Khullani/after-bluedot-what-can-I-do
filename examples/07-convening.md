# The AI Safety Debate — An Organizer's Playbook

> **Archetype #7, large public-event variant.** The core archetype is a small,
> output-oriented working session. This is the operational playbook for the
> other shape that has worked in practice: an 80–120-person public debate.

*How to run a high-signal, Oxford-style AI-safety debate in your city. Built
from the Chicago event (BlueDot Impact × Portal Innovations, June 2026) and
made to fork.*

**AI Safety Chicago** is a recurring Techne AI–BlueDot Impact debate series;
Portal Innovations was the venue and community partner for the June 2026
event. That event drew roughly 120 researchers, university staff, and
regulatory and legal professionals. Its event page is at
[luma.com/ais-chicago](https://luma.com/ais-chicago). At the time of the
original case study, a white-paper follow-up was planned and its first draft
was underway.

If you run a BlueDot group — or any local AI-safety community — a public
debate is one of the highest-leverage events you can put on. It draws people
who would never come to a lecture, surfaces real disagreements instead of
flattening them, and leaves the room genuinely moved. This is what I learned
running one, so you can run yours faster.

Fork it, copy the templates, and ignore what does not fit.

## Why a debate (and why this framing)

Most public AI events collapse into consensus or talk past each other. A
debate does the opposite: it forces the strongest version of each position
into the same room.

The framing that made ours work — and the one I would protect above all else:
**this is not believers versus dismissers.** It is serious people who *all*
take AI risk seriously and reach different conclusions about what to do. That
distinction is the whole point. It makes the event feel honest instead of
like a food fight, and it is what your moderator should reinforce from the
first minute.

The engine is a **before/after audience vote.** You measure where the room
stands before any argument, again at the end, and reveal the shift. That shift
— not a subjective declaration of “who won” — is the story. Movement in every
direction is a result.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Lead time** | ~8 weeks |
| **People** | 4 debaters (2 for / 2 against), 1 moderator, 3 volunteers |
| **Venue** | A partner space for 80–120, with AV |
| **Budget** | Mostly travel + printing; can be near zero with a venue partner |
| **Core tools** | RSVP platform, audience polling, post-event intake form, and partner/community channels |
| **The one non-negotiable** | The before/after vote |

## Choosing the motion

A good motion is **debatable, current, and open.**

- **Debatable:** smart people genuinely disagree. If one side is obviously
  right, it is a lecture.
- **Current:** it is live in the discourse this month.
- **Open:** leave the key term undefined so the debate can range. Ours —
  *“AI labs should pause development of frontier models until governance
  catches up”* — deliberately never defined *what kind* of pause (fixed-term,
  capability-triggered, governance-contingent, or permanent). That openness
  gave debaters room and kept the audience leaning in.

Write the voting prompt as a clean proposition — or prefix it with
“Resolved:” — so the vote maps cleanly to for/against.

## Recruiting debaters

**Structure:** two for, two against. Steel-man both sides. Recruit the people
who make the *strongest* version of each case, from a mix of institutions (a
research lab, think tanks across the spectrum, and an independent voice).
Institutional diversity signals the good-faith framing before anyone says a
word.

**Outreach:**

- Warm introductions beat cold email. Ask your network who they know on each
  side.
- Be specific about support. If you can cover travel, say what you can cover;
  if you cannot, say that just as plainly. Concrete offers get concrete
  answers.
- BlueDot's [Rapid Grants](https://bluedot.org/grants/rapid) currently include
  events and travel among eligible uses and offer up to $20,000. The
  live program page says BlueDot replies on average within a day and that nine
  in ten applicants hear back within a week. Check the page for current terms
  before making commitments.

**Build a bench.** Someone will cancel — ours did, on the day. Have a backup
for each side. In a true emergency the moderator can debate (I did), but only
if a prepared backup moderator or venue host takes over; nobody should
moderate a debate in which they are arguing a side. It is survivable; a deep
bench makes it a non-event.

The debater invitation and travel-support language are in
[`debate-emails.md`](../templates/debate-emails.md).

## Format and run of show

The complete run of show is about 83 minutes, with a ~75-minute
debate-and-vote spine after the welcome and introductions:

| Segment | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Opening vote | 2 min | Lock it; hide results |
| Opening statements | 6 min each | Establish the cases |
| Rebuttals | 3 min each | Respond directly |
| Moderated cross-examination | ~10 min | Moderator's prepared questions |
| Audience Q&A | ~15 min | Volunteer runs the mic |
| Closing statements | 2 min each | Last word before the revote |
| Final vote + reveal | ~4 min | The payoff |

Order matters: closings come *before* the final vote. That is what makes the
shift meaningful. Alternate sides in the speaking order. No slides and no
interruptions during speeches.

The full version, including transitions and cue lines, is in
[`debate-run-of-show.md`](../templates/debate-run-of-show.md).

## Moderation

The moderator makes or breaks it. The discipline:

- **Neutrality is everything.** Promotion can be provocative; the moderator
  cannot take a side.
- **Steer with questions, not opinions.** Draw out the strongest exchange,
  then get out of the way.
- **Steel-man and name the dodge.** Ask each side to state the other's best
  point. When someone evades, gently name it and re-ask.
- **Hold the motion open.** Do not let it collapse into a narrower question
  than the room voted on.

Prep deeply. The
[`debate-moderator-research-prompt.md`](../templates/debate-moderator-research-prompt.md)
builds a full landscape briefing; verify its citations, then turn it into a
one-page cue sheet for the night.

## Voting — the engine

Build **two identical polls** inside one event or presentation. Create poll
#1, then duplicate it; identical wording is critical.

The flow:

1. Open poll #1 at the top. Give about 30 seconds. **Lock it and hide the
   results** to avoid anchoring and make the reveal land.
2. After closings, open poll #2.
3. Compare the two results: *“Here's where we started — and here's where we
   landed.”* Use a built-in comparison view when available; otherwise reveal
   them sequentially or prepare a slide with the final numbers side by side.

Options should scale the position (Strongly support → Somewhat support →
Undecided → Somewhat oppose → Strongly oppose), so you can see movement, not
just a flip.

Tool limits change, so check the official plan page before the event. As of
September 2026:

- [Slido Basic](https://www.slido.com/pricing?plan=annual) supports up to 100
  joined participants and three polls per Slido. The participant cap is
  cumulative for that Slido, so create a fresh one for each event.
- [Mentimeter Free](https://help.mentimeter.com/en/articles/1258367-what-is-included-in-the-free-account)
  includes 50 participants per month. A live presentation can continue beyond
  that limit for up to eight hours, but the host cannot start another
  presentation after crossing the monthly cap until it resets or the account
  is upgraded. Keep both votes in the same presentation.
- Paid tiers do not all remove participant caps, and “monthly” prices may be
  billed annually. Check the current one-time/event plans rather than assuming
  a low-cost one-month upgrade exists.

**Rehearse the whole flow from a phone beforehand.** Use a small test group and
check how tests count against your plan. The reveal is too good to fumble live.

Full setup details are in
[`debate-voting-setup.md`](../templates/debate-voting-setup.md).

## Venue and logistics

A **venue partner** is the single biggest cost-saver. A startup hub,
university space, or coworking space will sometimes host for free in exchange
for visibility.

Nail down these details in writing (the venue-confirmation email is in
[`debate-emails.md`](../templates/debate-emails.md)):

- seating for your expected turnout + staging for four debaters and the
  moderator;
- lectern or lapel mics + a handheld for audience Q&A;
- one screen/TV for the opening and final votes;
- **photography and recording** — decide early, obtain permission, and respect
  any debater who only agrees to photos or to no capture at all; and
- setup access time + a sound check.

## Crew and roles

Four roles — three volunteers plus the moderator:

- **Check-in** — works the door with the roster.
- **Timekeeper** — runs the countdown and holds cue cards
  ([`debate-timekeeper-brief.md`](../templates/debate-timekeeper-brief.md)).
- **Q&A mic runner** — carries the mic and keeps questions to questions.
- **Moderator** — you or a dedicated host.

Brief the crew a week out and hand each person a one-page brief on the day.

## On-site assets

Generate and print:

- name badges, pre-filled from the RSVP export;
- an alphabetical check-in roster, with checkboxes + a walk-ins section;
- a poster for the entrance;
- table-tent cards for the debaters (name + affiliation); and
- a vote-screen graphic with your poll QR code and a short-link fallback.

Print at **100% / Actual size** (never “fit to page”) and do a plain-paper
alignment test before using label stock. These assets can be generated from an
RSVP CSV; a future contribution could add the generator to `templates/`.

## Sponsorship and budget

**Pitch the series, not the single event.** Asking a sponsor to back “a year of
AI-safety programming” can support a larger, more coherent partnership than a
one-off. Warm introductions dramatically outperform cold outreach.

A lean budget:

| Line item | Typical |
|---|---|
| Venue | $0 with a partner |
| Debater travel | $0–2,000, depending on who must travel |
| Catering / reception | Varies; a sponsor can cover it |
| Printing | ~$50–150 |
| Voting tool | $0 or the current price of an appropriate event plan |
| **Total** | **Often under a few hundred dollars before travel/catering** |

Use the editable budget in
[`debate-budget-and-checklists.md`](../templates/debate-budget-and-checklists.md).

## Promotion and RSVP

- Use an RSVP platform that handles reminders and offers a clean guest export
  for badges and the roster.
- Promote through the venue and other partners, your community channels, and
  LinkedIn. A provocative motion is your best marketing.
- Send reminders one week and one day before the event.

## Closing the loop (this is where the community gets built)

The event is the beginning, not the end. Within about two weeks:

- Send **1:1 thank-yous** to debaters, the venue, and sponsors — personal and
  by name.
- Send **an attendee thank-you**, naming the debaters and partners and sharing
  a few resources. Name individual attendees only with their consent. Use the
  event platform, a compliant mailing list, or BCC so recipients do not expose
  one another's addresses. Fold in a warm “we missed you” line for no-shows so
  one email can serve the whole list.
- Publish **a public recap** with a clear hook, short paragraphs, and resource
  links where the platform supports them.
- Send **a post-event intake form**. Ask what people want next *and* who is
  hiring, looking for a role, or building something that needs funding or
  collaborators. That is how a one-off becomes an ecosystem, and how you
  become an anchor for AI safety in your region.

The reusable copy is in
[`debate-emails.md`](../templates/debate-emails.md).

## When things go sideways

They will. From ours:

- **A debater cancels** → lean on your bench. The moderator can step in only
  if a backup moderator or venue host takes over the neutral role.
- **The weather turns** → some people will not make it. Acknowledge it warmly
  afterward and pull them toward the next one.
- **Tech fails** → cue cards work without power; keep a printed roster; rehearse
  voting so you can improvise.

None of these sank the night. Plan for them and they are footnotes.
