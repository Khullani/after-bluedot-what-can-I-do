# Example — Convening / panel / workshop

> Archetype #7.

## The artifact

**AI Safety Chicago** — [luma.com/ais-chicago](https://luma.com/ais-chicago)

- **Format:** First of a planned recurring series.
- **Hosting:** Co-hosted with **BlueDot Impact**.
- **Typical attendance:** ~120 individuals.
- **Audience composition:** Researchers and staff from the large Chicago
  universities; professionals from regulatory and legal employers in the
  region.
- **Post-event output:** A white paper follow-up to each event — see
  [`04-landscape-doc.md`](./04-landscape-doc.md) for the inaugural
  white paper.

## Why this is a useful example for the archetype

- **The output is named in the format.** Each event in the series
  produces a white paper. That commitment — made before the first event
  — is what separates this from a meetup. Without the named output, you
  have a party (see the failure-modes section of
  [archetype #7](../archetypes/07-convening.md)).
- **Co-hosting with BlueDot Impact compounds credibility for both
  sides.** BlueDot's brand brings serious researchers; Techne AI's local
  network brings regulatory and legal practitioners. Neither side could
  pull 120 of *this specific mix* alone.
- **The audience is the asset.** Universities + regulatory/legal
  practitioners is a deliberately cross-cutting room — research meets
  practice in the same building, which most AI-safety convenings do
  not. That mix is what the white paper output captures.
- **Recurring series, not one-off.** The series format is what lets the
  white paper output compound. Each successive paper cites prior ones;
  the alumni network across events becomes a working group.

## The combination it demonstrates

This pairs **archetype #7 (convening) with archetype #4 (landscape doc)**
— exactly the combination named in
[`PLAYBOOK.md`](../PLAYBOOK.md#combinations-that-work). The convening
gathers the practitioners; the white paper is the artifact they pressure-
test together; the next convening uses the paper as the pre-read.

## What I'd do differently next time

- Pre-circulate a short "questions we want answered" document before
  event #2 so attendees come with positions, not just curiosity.
- Add a small (8–10 person) closed working session immediately
  before each public event — the public event handles 120, the closed
  session drafts the white paper.
