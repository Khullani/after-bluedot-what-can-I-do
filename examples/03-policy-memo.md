# Example — Policy memo / staff bill analysis

> Archetype #3, **staff bill analysis** variant.

## The artifact

[`HB5760-staff-bill-analysis.pdf`](./HB5760-staff-bill-analysis.pdf) — a
5-page section-by-section analysis of **Illinois HB5760, the Commission
on Artificial Intelligence and Workforce Transition Act**, sponsored by
Rep. Kimberly Du Buclet, introduced May 5, 2026, in the 104th General
Assembly.

The bill establishes a 20-member Commission to direct seven State
agencies to conduct occupational, industry, regional, and demographic
assessments of AI-driven labor disruption in Illinois. It is
deliberately non-regulatory of private AI — it directs the State's own
planning and workforce-development apparatus, positioning the bill
within the carve-out for state government use of AI in Executive Order
14365.

## Who received it, and when

Delivered to **Rep. Du Buclet's office** as a working reference for the
sponsor and committee staff. **Follow-up meetings are in flight at the
time of this writing.**

## Why this is a useful example for the archetype

A few things this artifact does that I'd point cohort members to:

- **It's the staff-analysis variant, not the recommendation-memo
  variant.** The audience is committee staff and members reading the
  bill section by section, not a single decision-maker being asked for
  a vote. It opens with neutral "what the bill does / why now / what
  the bill does NOT do" framing before the section-by-section walk.
- **"What the bill does NOT do" earns trust early.** Listing the things
  the bill is *not* doing — no private-sector regulation, no private
  right of action, no federal privacy preemption — heads off the
  reflexive objections that would otherwise dominate the first
  conversation.
- **Design rationale is named in each section.** For example, Section
  10's frontier-AI threshold (10²⁶ FLOPs) is placed in statute rather
  than cross-referenced to another pending bill — making HB5760
  self-contained. The analysis names the choice, not just the
  provision. Staff use that kind of design-rationale framing when
  briefing members.
- **The "Note on changes from the discussion draft" section.** If
  there's a prior public draft, naming what changed and why
  short-circuits a lot of stakeholder confusion.

## Template

The staff bill analysis structure used here is captured as a reusable
scaffold in
[`../templates/staff-bill-analysis.md`](../templates/staff-bill-analysis.md).
The HB5760 analysis adapts that structure — with the addition that the
template ends in a **recommendation section** for cases where the staff
analysis is also doing advocacy work, which is the variant I'd default
to.

## What I'd do differently next time

- Pair the analysis with a 2-page recommendation memo
  ([`../templates/one-pager.md`](../templates/one-pager.md)) for any
  individual legislator who needs the TL;DR without the full walkthrough.
  Same research, different artifact, different audience.
- Append a one-paragraph fiscal-note response if a fiscal note is
  pending — staff appreciate getting both at once.
