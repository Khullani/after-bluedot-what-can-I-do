# Example — Landscape doc

> Archetype #4.

## The artifact

**DNA synthesis landscape document** — a public field-building writeup
mapping an under-covered area at the intersection of AI policy and
biosecurity.

- **Link:** [Google Doc — DNA synthesis landscape document](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1w-KqzvdN-GqH6pA4I0koqYnKEySwKHtS_945Bgp-fXk/edit?tab=t.0#heading=h.2gsm4b1fvo18)
- **Status:** Published by BlueDot Impact.
- **Genre:** Field-building landscape document — synthesizes adjacent
  literatures (AI policy, biosecurity, genomics) into a primer the
  field can cite as the starting point.

## Methodology

This doc follows BlueDot Impact's
**[field strategy playbook](https://blog.bluedot.org/p/field-strategy-playbook)** —
the framework BlueDot uses to deliberately build a research/practice
field where one doesn't yet exist. The playbook treats a landscape doc
not as literature review but as **field-building infrastructure**: it
gives later contributors a shared map so their work compounds rather
than duplicating effort.

## Why this is a useful example for the archetype

- **The gap is named, not waved at.** "Where AI policy intersects with
  DNA synthesis governance" is a specific cross-section nobody else has
  mapped publicly. Archetype #4's bar — *you can name in one sentence
  the gap your doc fills* — is met.
- **Field-building, not just describing.** The BlueDot playbook framing
  means the doc is built to be *built on*: explicit scope, methodology,
  and invitations for others to fill named gaps. That's the difference
  between a landscape doc that gets cited and one that gets read once.
- **Adjacent-literature synthesis.** AI policy, biosecurity, and genomics
  each have substantial literatures; the synthesis between them does
  not. That's exactly the "under-covered angle" framing archetype #4
  calls for.
