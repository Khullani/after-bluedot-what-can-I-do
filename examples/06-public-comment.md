# Example — Public comment / testimony

> Archetype #6.

## The artifact

**Written testimony**, hosted at
[strategy.techne.ai/illinois-AI-laws/testimony.html](https://strategy.techne.ai/illinois-AI-laws/testimony.html).

The page *is* the testimony itself, not an index — the body that received
it, the date submitted, and the full text are on the page. Visit the
link for the canonical version.

## Context

The testimony sits inside the broader
[illinois-AI-laws](https://github.com/Khullani/illinois-AI-laws) tracker
project (archetype #1). That pairing matters: the tracker is the
substrate that gives the testimony its citations and its institutional
context. The testimony, in turn, is one of the concrete things the
tracker exists *for* — a tracker without a downstream use is just a
spreadsheet.

This is the
[**#1 (tracker) → #6 (public comment) combination** named in
`PLAYBOOK.md`](../PLAYBOOK.md#combinations-that-work).

## Why this is a useful example for the archetype

- **The testimony is co-located with the tracker.** Hosting it on the
  same domain as the underlying legislative tracker makes the citations
  one-click — a member or staffer reading the testimony can click
  through to the bill page in the same browser tab.
- **It's *written* testimony.** Submitting in writing alongside (or
  instead of) oral testimony is the under-supplied move. Oral testimony
  has a short tail; written testimony enters the legislative record and
  gets re-read.
- **The page is the artifact, not a PDF download.** Web-native testimony
  is more linkable, more shareable, and more discoverable than a PDF
  attachment to a hearing record.

## What I'd do differently next time

- Add a short summary at the top of the testimony page so readers in a
  hurry can get the position in 30 seconds.
- Cross-link the testimony from the bill's page in the tracker — both
  directions, so the tracker page shows "testimony submitted by X" and
  the testimony shows "this argues for [position] on [bill]."
