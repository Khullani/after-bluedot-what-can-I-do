# Example — Op-ed / public writing

> Archetype #5.

## The artifact

**"AI legislation must focus on biggest risks"** (display title:
*Opinion: Illinois can lead with smart, targeted AI regulation*)

- **Outlet:** *Crain's Chicago Business*, Commentary section
- **Published:** March 30, 2026 (4:21 PM CDT)
- **Link:** [chicagobusiness.com/opinion/commentary/ccb-opinion-ai-legislation-20260330](https://www.chicagobusiness.com/opinion/commentary/ccb-opinion-ai-legislation-20260330/)
- **Byline:** Khullani Abdullahi, CEO of Techne AI in Chicago

## The argument

Illinois lawmakers should pass **HB 4705 / SB 3261** (the Artificial
Intelligence Public Safety & Child Protection Transparency Act), which
*surgically targets only the largest AI companies*, because it best
balances public safety with innovation.

## The named decision-maker

**Illinois state legislators in Springfield** — specifically members
considering HB 4705 / SB 3261 during the 104th General Assembly.

## Why this is a useful example for the archetype

- **The hook is a live bill.** The piece runs because a vote is coming,
  not because AI is generally important. Editors run op-eds tied to
  decisions.
- **One argument, not three.** "Surgical regulation that targets the
  largest companies" is a single, sharp claim. The piece doesn't try
  to also cover safety techniques, international comparisons, or
  long-horizon risk.
- **The byline carries operational credibility.** "CEO of Techne AI in
  Chicago" + the opening self-description ("I run Techne AI, a
  Chicago-based consultancy that focuses on AI governance, risk and
  compliance") signals that the author works in the actual market the
  bill regulates. That's what makes a section editor take a piece at
  this level of specificity.
- **The target is named at the state level.** Springfield, Illinois
  General Assembly, this session. Not "policymakers."

## What I'd do differently next time

- Pair the op-ed with a 2-page recommendation memo to the bill's
  sponsors and committee chairs — same argument, different audience.
  The op-ed builds public air cover; the memo does the close.
- Write a follow-up piece in 4–6 weeks if the bill moves. Editors run
  follow-ups when there's a procedural development to hook to.
