# Example — Draft a bill

> Archetype #12.

## The artifact

**IL HB5760 — Commission on Artificial Intelligence and Workforce
Transition Act**, a bill in the 104th Illinois General Assembly
establishing a state commission to assess the impact of generative and
frontier AI systems on Illinois industries, occupations, regions, and
workers, and to recommend updates to workforce-development, education,
apprenticeship, unemployment-insurance, and income-support programs.

- **Link:** [billtrack50.com/billdetail/2002509](https://www.billtrack50.com/billdetail/2002509) — also via the [Illinois General Assembly](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/BillStatus?DocNum=5760&GAID=18&DocTypeID=HB&SessionID=114&GA=104)
- **Sponsor:** Kimberly du Buclet (D), Illinois House
- **Introduced:** April 30, 2026
- **Last action:** Referred to Rules Committee, May 5, 2026
- **Sunset:** Repeals January 1, 2034 (built-in review point)
- **Status:** Introduced; in committee.

## What the bill does

- **Creates the Commission on Artificial Intelligence and Workforce
  Transition.**
- **Directs specified State agencies** to conduct assessments of the
  actual and projected impact of generative AI systems and frontier AI
  systems on Illinois industries, occupations, regions, and workers.
- **Requires the Commission to identify, review, and make
  recommendations** on existing State and federal workforce
  development, education, apprenticeship, unemployment-insurance, and
  income-support programs in light of those assessments.
- **Requires designated senior staff liaisons** from named State
  agencies to support the Commission's work.
- **Mandates an initial comprehensive report and biennial updates**
  with findings and recommendations.
- **Sets provisions** for Commission membership, administrative
  support, meetings, and confidentiality.

## Why this is a useful example for the archetype

- **The sponsor is named and committed.** Without Rep. du Buclet's
  office, the bill is unfiled text. The sponsor relationship is the
  gating constraint for archetype #12 — and this example demonstrates
  it.
- **The legislative function is appropriately scoped.** The bill
  doesn't try to regulate AI outright; it creates a coordinating
  Commission to *assess and recommend*. That's the right shape for a
  first AI-workforce bill in a state that doesn't yet have AI-specific
  labor statute. Bills that try to regulate AI directly tend to die
  in committee; bills that create study commissions tend to move.
- **The bill targets a coordination gap that regulation alone can't
  fill.** Individual State agencies already have authority to study
  individual programs. What's missing is a single body coordinating
  the AI-impact assessment across all of them and feeding the findings
  into program updates. That coordinating function is exactly what
  legislation is for.
- **It carries a sunset clause.** Repeal on January 1, 2034 — the bill
  builds in its own review point. Good legislative hygiene for AI
  policy where the technology moves faster than statute.
- **It demonstrates the brief-to-bill pipeline.** The substantive case
  (rapid AI-induced workforce change requires coordinated State
  response) was made first; the bill is the artifact that the brief
  earned. This is the **#1 (tracker) / #3 (memo) → #12 (bill)**
  combination in action.

## What I'd do differently next time

- Pair the introduction with a **2-page brief** sent to every member of
  the committee of referral, not just the sponsor. Committee chairs
  schedule hearings on bills they understand; the brief is the
  shortcut.
- Co-introduce in the **Senate** alongside the House version (already
  done for many AI bills; worth doing here too). Companion bills move
  faster than single-chamber bills.
- Add a **public comment period** for the Commission's initial report,
  built into the bill itself — turns the report into a vehicle for
  archetype #6 follow-on.
