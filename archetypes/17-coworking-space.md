# Archetype 17: AI safety coworking space (member, not visitor)

> Apply to a curated AI safety coworking space and spend 2–3 days a week there for at least three months. Sustained co-location, not a one-off visit.

## Why this archetype exists

Almost everything else on this menu is an artifact you produce alone and
then share. This one is different: the artifact is *proximity*. AI safety
coworking spaces — LISA in London, Constellation and FAR Labs in Berkeley,
and a growing set elsewhere — concentrate researchers, org staff, and
programme fellows in one room. The people you'd otherwise spend a year
trying to reach one cold email at a time are getting lunch at the next
table.

The reason to make this its own archetype rather than folding it into
"networking" is that the compounding comes from *sustained* co-location,
not from showing up once. A member who's there twice a week for three
months becomes a known quantity: people learn what you're working on,
pull you into conversations, vouch for you when a role opens. A visitor
who drops in for a day gets a nice lunch and leaves no trace.

## When to pick this

**Best fit for:** any of the four BlueDot courses (FAIG, AGIS, BIOS, TAIS) — densest for TAIS and AGIS, but governance and biosecurity researchers are welcome members at every major hub.

- You **live near, or can relocate to, a hub** — London (LISA),
  Berkeley (Constellation, FAR Labs), or another city with a curated
  AI safety space. Or you can afford a multi-week visiting stint.
- You **already have something you're working on** — a technical
  follow-up (#16), a landscape doc (#4), a memo (#3), a fellowship
  application (#10). The space accelerates work in progress; it doesn't
  substitute for having work.
- You can commit **2–3 days a week for at least three months**. Less
  than that and you never cross from "new face" to "known member."
- You're **energized by working alongside people**, not drained by an
  open-plan room full of strangers.

## When NOT to pick this

- **You have nothing in progress.** The space rewards people who are
  visibly building. Show up with a project (ship a #16, #4, or #3
  first), or you'll rattle around feeling like an impostor.
- **You'd treat membership as the achievement.** Getting accepted is
  not the win. Membership without engagement — showing up, headphones
  on, talking to no one — is the single most common way to waste the
  space.
- **You can only manage occasional drop-ins.** That's archetype #14
  (networking events), not this. The value here is specifically the
  sustained presence.
- **You're not near a hub and can't travel.** Set up or join a local
  chapter (#15) instead, and use 1:1 outreach (#13) to stay connected
  to the hubs remotely.

## What v1 looks like

1. **An application submitted** to a specific space (LISA,
   Constellation, FAR Labs, or another), naming what you're currently
   working on and why co-location helps it.
2. **Accepted**, and a **regular cadence established** — the same 2–3
   days each week, so people learn to expect you.
3. **First month of sustained desk-time** logged: you're a face people
   recognize, not a guest badge.
4. **At least one substantive collaboration or feedback loop** with
   another member — a paper read, a draft critiqued, a project
   co-scoped, an intro made.

You're done with v1 when you've been a regular for a month and one real
working relationship has formed that wouldn't have existed otherwise.

## What to do after v1

- **Convert the desk-time into an artifact.** Use the proximity to
  ship a #16 technical follow-up, pressure-test a #4 landscape doc, or
  sharpen a #10 fellowship application with feedback from people who've
  done the fellowship.
- **Host something small** — a reading group or a lightning-talk lunch
  (a lightweight #7 convening) — inside the space. Members who organize
  become central fast.
- **Use the network for the next move.** Most people who spend a
  sustained stint at a hub leave with a role, a collaborator, or a
  fellowship they wouldn't have gotten cold.

## Where to look

- **[LISA — London Initiative for Safe AI](https://www.safeai.org.uk/)**
  — members-led hub in central London hosting Apollo Research, ARENA,
  a MATS extension, and BlueDot Impact, plus many individual
  researchers. Open to membership applications, including short
  visiting stints and once-or-twice-a-week arrangements.
- **[Constellation](https://constellation.org/)** — Berkeley research
  center with ~150 network participants, shared meals, and ongoing
  convenings. Access via the [Astra Fellowship](https://constellation.org/programs/astra)
  or the Visiting Researcher Program.
- **[FAR Labs](https://www.far.ai/labs/)** — downtown Berkeley
  coworking hub, 40+ members, welcoming to technical and governance
  researchers plus field-building and advocacy. Hosts visitors up to
  four weeks free; longer memberships available.
- **Elsewhere** — new hubs keep opening. Ask in your cohort's channel
  or the hubs above whether there's one in your city.

## How I'd start if I were you (week 1)

1. **Pick the hub that matches your location and your work.** Don't
   apply to all three; pick the one you could actually be at twice a
   week (or the one worth a multi-week trip).
2. **Name what you're working on** in one paragraph — the project
   co-location would accelerate. If you can't name it, ship a #16 /
   #4 / #3 first, then apply.
3. **Submit the application.** Most hubs have a rolling form. Reference
   the specific project and the cadence you're proposing.
4. **Plan the cadence before you're accepted.** Which two days? Block
   them now so acceptance turns into presence, not intention.

## Common failure modes

- **Membership without engagement.** The biggest one. Being in the room
  is necessary, not sufficient — the value is in the conversations, and
  those require you to start them.
- **Treating acceptance as the finish line.** Acceptance is week zero.
  The archetype is the three months after.
- **Visiting instead of belonging.** A day here, a day there, never the
  same day twice — you stay a stranger. Pick a cadence and hold it.
- **Showing up with nothing to build.** People connect over work. With
  no project, you have nothing to talk about and no reason for anyone
  to remember you.
- **Overrelocating on a hunch.** Don't move across an ocean before a
  visiting stint tells you the hub is a fit. Most spaces do short
  visits precisely so you can test before you commit.

## Combinations

- **#13 (1:1 outreach) / #14 (networking events) → #17** — outreach and
  event attendance are how you get on the radar of members who can
  vouch for your application.
- **#17 → #16 (technical follow-up)** — the conversations and feedback
  in the space are what turn a vague research interest into a scoped,
  shippable replication.
- **#10 (fellowship) ⟷ #17** — two routes to the same rooms. A
  fellowship (MATS, ARENA, Astra) is the *structured-programme* route
  into a hub; coworking membership is the *self-directed* route for
  people who already have work and want the proximity without the
  programme.
