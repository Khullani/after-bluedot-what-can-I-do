# Archetype 6: Public comment / regulatory submission

> Respond to an open RFC, NIST draft, agency notice, or rulemaking comment
> period in your area of interest.

## Why this archetype exists

Public comments are one of the most under-supplied high-leverage moves in
AI governance right now. Agencies are required to read them. They get
cited in final rulemakings. The supply of substantive, well-argued
comments — especially from non-industry voices — is absurdly thin compared
to the leverage available. Three or four serious comments on a federal
docket can shift the framing of a final rule.

This is also the cheapest archetype on the menu: the audience is built in,
the deadline is built in, the publication venue is built in. You just have
to write the thing.

## When to pick this

**Best fit for:** FAIG, AGIS, BIOS — most public-comment windows are policy / governance; TAIS only when there's a technical-standards comment open.

- There is an open comment window **right now** in your area of interest.
  Check regulations.gov, the Federal Register, NIST drafts, FTC dockets,
  state agencies, EU consultations.
- You have 10–20 hours of writing time over 2–4 weeks.
- You can take a clear position. Hedged comments are ignored; sharp
  ones are read.

## When NOT to pick this

- There isn't an open window. Do not manufacture one. Note the upcoming
  windows in your calendar, do another archetype in the meantime, and
  come back when a real comment opens.
- You feel obligated to address every question the agency asked. You
  don't. Pick the 2–3 questions where you have something useful to
  say, ignore the rest.
- You think public comments are pointless. Read three final
  rulemakings and see how often comments get cited. They are not
  pointless.

## What v1 looks like

A 3–8 page comment, submitted before the deadline, that:

1. Identifies you and your relevant background (1 paragraph). Affiliation
   helps but isn't required — independent commenters get read too.
2. States your position in the first paragraph. No throat-clearing.
3. Addresses 2–4 specific questions or sections of the agency's notice,
   by number, with substantive recommendations.
4. Provides citations and evidence — not academic-style, but specific.
5. Concludes with a clean "we recommend X" statement.

You're done when it's filed and you have the receipt / docket entry
number.

## What v2/v3 looks like

- A short companion blog post or op-ed (archetype #5) summarizing your
  comment for a public audience.
- A coalition comment: same submission, co-signed by 4–8 other
  practitioners. Coalitions get read more carefully.
- A practice of commenting consistently — same author name on the
  docket for a year — which builds standing with agency staff.

## How I'd start if I were you (week 1)

1. **Inventory open comment windows in your area.** Spend an hour. Pick
   the one with the highest leverage and a deadline at least 2 weeks
   out.
2. **Read the agency's notice in full.** Note the specific questions
   they asked. You'll respond to 2–4, not all.
3. **Draft your position in one sentence.** "We recommend that NIST
   [do X / not do Y / clarify Z]." If you can't write the sentence,
   you're not ready.
4. **Outline by their question numbers.** Each of your 2–4 picks gets
   one section.

## Common failure modes

- **Hedging.** Comments full of "we appreciate the agency's
  thoughtful consideration" and "further study is warranted" go in the
  pile. Stake a claim.
- **Trying to answer every question.** The notice often has 30+
  questions. Pick 2–4. Ignore the rest. The agency wants depth on
  specifics, not shallow coverage.
- **Missing the deadline.** Federal dockets do not extend. Set your
  internal deadline 5 days before the real one.
