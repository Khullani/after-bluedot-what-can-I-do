# Archetype 9: Apply for a specific role

> One named role at one named organization, with the same investment you'd give a memo.

## Why this archetype exists

Most people who finish a course and start thinking about "AI safety jobs"
stall on the meta-question — which roles, which org, am I qualified — and
ship zero applications. The fix isn't more research. The fix is picking
*one* role you'd genuinely take, treating the application as a project,
and putting the same 20–30 hours into it that you'd put into a landscape
doc.

Career moves and the rest of the menu compound: a landscape doc, tracker,
or op-ed is what makes your application non-generic. Applying for a role
is also a finishable archetype on its own.

## When to pick this

**Best fit for:** any of the four BlueDot courses (FAIG, AGIS, BIOS, TAIS).

- You can name the org and the role specifically — not "any AI safety
  job."
- You have 4 weeks for focused work: role research, two or three
  informational conversations, drafting, multiple revisions.
- You have a portfolio item that maps to the role: a memo (#3), landscape
  doc (#4), op-ed (#5), tracker (#1), or testimony (#6). If you don't,
  ship one of those first.
- You can identify at least one current or former employee who'd give
  you 30 minutes.

## When NOT to pick this

- You're applying out of inertia. If you wouldn't take the role, you're
  using applications to procrastinate.
- You have no prior work that demonstrates the skills the job needs.
  Ship one of the writing archetypes first.
- "AI safety" is your filter rather than a specific org and role.
  Without specificity, the application reads generic.

## What v1 looks like

A single complete application to one specific role:

1. Job posting bookmarked; requirements understood in detail, not
   skimmed.
2. Resume tailored to *this* role's actual requirements.
3. Cover letter that names specific work you've done — with links —
   that maps to specific needs in the JD.
4. Two or three informational conversations completed with people
   inside or recently inside the org.
5. Application submitted, ideally with a referral.

You're done with v1 when the application is in. Done well when you got
an interview.

## What to do after v1

- **If you advance:** prep specifically for *this* org's interview
  format. Re-read the JD; ask the people you talked to what the team
  optimizes for.
- **If you don't:** ask the recruiter or hiring manager for one piece
  of feedback. Use the same portfolio item to apply to two adjacent
  roles.
- **Either way:** publish what you learned (archetype #11) — the
  reflection is signal for the next round.

## Where to look

[`jobs.80000hours.org`](https://jobs.80000hours.org/) is the canonical job
board for impact-focused roles, including AI safety policy and research.
Filter by problem area (AI safety), role type, seniority, and location.
Most serious orgs post here.

## How I'd start if I were you (week 1)

1. **List 5 roles, narrow to 1.** Pick the one where you can name
   (a) why this org and (b) what skills you'd bring that the team needs.
2. **Identify two contacts.** Current employees, recent alumni, or
   someone in your network who knows the team. Send the asks now; they
   take time to land.
3. **Read the JD carefully.** Take notes on every requirement. Identify
   which of your prior work maps to which.
4. **Draft the cover letter.** Specific, evidence-based, no boilerplate.
   Send a draft to the people you talked to for feedback.

## Common failure modes

- **Applying broadly instead of deeply.** One application with 20 hours
  of work outperforms ten with two hours each. Pick the role and invest.
- **Skipping the informational conversations.** This is where you find
  out what the role really is, who the team is, what they're optimizing
  for. Skip it and you're competing with people who didn't.
- **Treating the application as the portfolio.** The portfolio is what
  you did *before* you applied (the memo, the tracker, the landscape
  doc). The application packages that.
