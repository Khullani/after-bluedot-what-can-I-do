# Archetype 4: Landscape Doc on an Under-Covered Topic

> A public writeup that fills a gap in the literature nobody else has filled.

## Why this archetype exists

Some topics in AI governance are conspicuously under-mapped: evals for a
specific domain (healthcare, criminal justice, education), governance gaps
in a specific sector, the regulatory posture of a specific country.
Anybody who needs to think seriously about that topic ends up reading the
same five Substack posts and the same two academic papers, and then
googling around.

If you can produce the canonical landscape doc on one of these gaps, you
become the citation. The doc gets pulled into other people's work for
years. This is the archetype with the longest tail.

## When to pick this

- You have research instincts and the discipline to scope tightly.
- You can name in one sentence the gap your doc fills. "There's no
  comprehensive overview of how state-level Medicaid agencies are using
  algorithmic eligibility tools" is a gap. "AI in healthcare" is not.
- You have 4–10 hours a week for 8–12 weeks.
- You can resist the urge to make it perfect before publishing.

## When NOT to pick this

- You can't name the gap. If your one-sentence pitch starts "there's
  not enough writing on…" the gap is too broad — and the writing
  probably exists, you just haven't found it yet. Spend a week
  searching before you commit.
- You can't tolerate publishing a v1 that's imperfect. Landscape docs
  ship in iterations.
- You want fast credibility. Landscape docs take 2–3 months to land.
  Pick #3 or #5 if you need faster signal.

## What v1 looks like

A 3,000–8,000 word public document (Substack, personal site, or PDF on
a project page) that:

1. Names the gap and why it matters (1–2 paragraphs).
2. Defines scope crisply — what's in, what's out, time window, geography.
3. Methodology — how you collected and synthesized sources.
4. Findings — organized into 4–8 clear sections.
5. Gaps you couldn't fill yourself, as an invitation for others.
6. An appendix with the source list.

You're done with v1 when somebody who works in that area cites it
unprompted.

## What v2/v3 looks like

- A version 2 incorporating responses from practitioners who emailed
  you after v1.
- A small convening (archetype #7) to pressure-test the next version.
- A short op-ed (archetype #5) drawn from one of the findings, to bring
  more readers to the long version.
- A sustained beat: quarterly updates as the landscape evolves.

## Example in the wild

[`examples/04-landscape-doc.md`](../examples/04-landscape-doc.md) —
the inaugural white paper from the AI Safety Chicago event series
(co-hosted by Techne AI and BlueDot Impact). Pressure-tested by ~120
practitioners from Chicago universities and regulatory/legal employers
at the convening, then drafted into a public landscape doc. This is the
**#7 (convening) → #4 (landscape doc) combination** in action.

Good external models for solo desk-research landscape docs: AI Now
Institute's annual reports, GovAI's policy briefs, the AI Index. Notice
what they all do: tight scope, clear methodology, opinion declared.

## How I'd start if I were you (week 1)

1. **Write the one-sentence gap pitch.** Send it to two people in the
   field. If both say "isn't that already covered by X?" search X,
   read it, and either refine your pitch or pick a different gap.
2. **Spend the rest of the week reading.** Build the source list
   before you write a word.
3. **Draft the table of contents.** Section headings only. This is
   your scope contract with yourself.
4. **Pick the publishing venue** before you start writing. Substack,
   personal site, GitHub Pages. Decide now to avoid the 2-week
   stall at the end.

## Common failure modes

- **Scope expanding mid-research.** You'll find adjacent rabbit holes
  every week. Note them for v2, do not chase them in v1.
- **Treating this like a literature review.** Landscape docs have a
  point of view. Yours should too.
- **Polishing forever.** Publish v1 at the 80% mark. The feedback you
  get is more valuable than the last 20% of polish.
