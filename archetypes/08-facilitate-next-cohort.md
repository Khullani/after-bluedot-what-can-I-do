# Archetype 8: Facilitate the Next Cohort

> Run a BlueDot course — the same one you took, or a local adaptation —
> for a new group.

## Why this archetype exists

The fastest way to deepen your own grasp of the curriculum is to teach it.
The fastest way to compound the field is to add a new facilitator. Both
happen at once if you facilitate a cohort. And — this is the part that
gets overlooked — facilitating signals to BlueDot and to your local AI
governance network that you're someone who shows up reliably, which opens
doors that one-off projects don't.

The honest caveat: this archetype is real work, not a default. People who
pick it because it feels safe and familiar burn out. People who pick it
because facilitation is genuinely energizing for them tend to compound
across multiple cohorts.

## When to pick this

- You enjoyed the facilitator role in your own cohort — speaking up,
  shaping the conversation, holding the room. You can tell the
  difference between facilitating well and just talking a lot.
- You have 6–10 hours a week for 10–12 weeks for a single cohort.
- You have a credible recruitment pipeline: 12–20 people in your
  network who could be candidates.
- You're willing to do the unglamorous work — logistics, follow-ups,
  no-shows, content prep.

## When NOT to pick this

- You'd be doing it because it's the most legible thing to do after a
  course. Facilitation is a calling, not a default.
- You aren't excited about the curriculum anymore. Facilitators have
  to be the most engaged person in the room.
- You'd rather write or research than hold a room.

## What v1 looks like

A full cohort run — either via BlueDot's facilitator pipeline or as a
local adaptation:

1. 8–12 participants recruited and vetted.
2. 8–12 weekly sessions, 90–120 minutes each, run on schedule.
3. Reading assignments completed, discussions facilitated, written
   assignments graded or peer-reviewed.
4. A capstone project from each participant.
5. A retrospective and a written reflection from you on what worked
   and what didn't.

You're done with v1 when the cohort finishes and at least two
participants pick an archetype from this menu and start it.

## What v2/v3 looks like

- A second cohort with alumni from the first as TAs.
- A local adaptation of the curriculum (which becomes archetype #2 at
  a larger scale).
- Becoming a paid BlueDot facilitator if you've run two successful
  cohorts.
- A community of practice across facilitators in your region.

## Example in the wild

BlueDot's facilitator pipeline is the official route — apply through
their site after your first cohort. The local-adaptation route is
documented in
[agi-strategy-government](https://github.com/Khullani/agi-strategy-government),
which is a 5-week salon version rather than a full course but
illustrates the adaptation pattern.

## How I'd start if I were you (week 1)

1. **Apply to BlueDot's facilitator pipeline** if you're going the
   official route. It's competitive and slow; start the application
   the week after your cohort ends.
2. **If you're running a local adaptation, scope it.** Length,
   participants, curriculum slice. See archetype #2 for the salon
   version.
3. **Pre-recruit informally.** Talk to 5 candidates before you
   commit. If two or three are excited, you have momentum. If
   nobody is, the cohort isn't there yet.
4. **Block the calendar.** Facilitation requires reliable weekly
   commitment. If your calendar can't hold it, don't pick this
   archetype.

## Common failure modes

- **Underestimating the prep time.** A 90-minute session takes 3–5
  hours of prep, especially the first time through the material.
- **Treating facilitation as content delivery.** It's not — the
  participants do the work. Your job is structure, pacing, and
  drawing out the quiet voices.
- **Not running a retrospective.** Without one, the lessons from
  cohort one don't compound into cohort two.
- **Facilitating to feel useful.** If you're not energized after
  sessions, this archetype isn't for you and there's no shame in
  picking a different one.
