# Archetype 10: Apply for a fellowship

> Pick one fellowship that matches your trajectory. Apply seriously. Treat the application like an archetype, not a backup plan.

## Why this archetype exists

Fellowships compress what would otherwise take years of self-direction
into a stipended, mentored 3–12 weeks. They put you next to senior
researchers, give you a deliverable to ship, and route you into the
network. After a BlueDot course, this is the single biggest force
multiplier *if* the fellowship matches what you actually want to do
next — and the application itself, done well, is a finishable archetype.

The bar for a strong application is roughly the bar for a strong memo
or landscape doc: a specific research question, a credible plan for the
time, and demonstrated prior work.

## When to pick this

**Best fit for:** any of the four BlueDot courses — fellowships skew different ways (MATS / ARENA / SPAR / Apart toward TAIS; GovAI / IAPS / Talos toward governance; ERA bridges both).

- A specific fellowship's focus, timing, and location match your
  trajectory. "I want to do AI safety research" is not enough; "I want
  to spend 10 weeks at LISA on interpretability mentored by someone
  working on X" is.
- You have 3–6 weeks for the application: a clear project pitch, a
  research statement, the formal application form, and conversations
  with prior fellows.
- You can describe what you'd do in your three months in concrete terms.
- You have at least one piece of public work demonstrating the skills
  the fellowship trains.

## When NOT to pick this

- The fellowship's timing doesn't fit. Don't apply to a summer cohort
  if the timing requires you to quit a job that's actually working.
- You're applying because it sounds prestigious. Fellowships are
  commitments; the application is real work.
- You haven't shipped anything publicly yet. Ship a landscape doc or
  memo first — the application will be stronger and you'll know whether
  research or policy is actually what you want.

## What v1 looks like

A single submitted fellowship application:

1. The fellowship picked. One, with a clear reason why *this* one over
   the others below.
2. Project proposal written: what you'd work on for the 3–12 weeks, in
   concrete enough terms that a mentor could push back substantively.
3. Research / policy statement: a 1–2 page narrative connecting your
   trajectory to the fellowship's focus.
4. Conversations completed with at least one prior fellow and (if
   possible) one current or former mentor at the org.
5. Application submitted before the deadline, not in the final 6 hours.

You're done with v1 when the application is in. Done well when you
advance to interview.

## The most-cited AI-safety / AI-governance fellowships

Listed alphabetically; pick by fit, not prestige. Cohort timing and
application status change frequently — verify on the official site
before you commit.

### [AI Safety Camp](https://www.aisafety.camp/)

Distributed online team-based research program. Opening weekend mid-January;
projects January–April; final presentations late April. Apply Nov 1–23. Lower-cost
entry point than the residentials.
**Fit:** people who want a real research project they can do remote and
part-time.

### [Apart Research — Apart Lab Fellowship](https://apartresearch.com/)

8–16 week fully remote research fellowship focused on developing
promising ideas into polished outputs through mentorship and the
research-sprint format. 100+ researchers supported.
**Fit:** people with a specific research idea and the discipline to
make it polished asynchronously.

### [ARENA — Alignment Research Engineer Accelerator](https://www.arena.education/)

Technical training program at the London Initiative for Safe AI (LISA).
ARENA 8.0 ran May 25 – June 26, 2026. Focused on the engineering skills
empirical-alignment work requires.
**Fit:** technically-capable people who want to skill up on the
alignment-engineer side before MATS or a research role.

### [ERA — Cambridge Existential Risk Alliance](https://erafellowship.org/)

10-week summer fellowship in Cambridge, UK. Salary equivalent £34,125
prorated, accommodation, meals, visa support. Technical and governance
research, especially projects that bridge both. 85+ alumni across 10+
countries since 2021.
**Fit:** researchers who want a Cambridge cohort and projects that
unite technical and policy work.

### [GovAI — Centre for the Governance of AI](https://www.governance.ai/)

3-month Summer and Winter Fellowships in Oxford, UK. £9,000 stipend
plus travel. Research and Policy tracks. Welcomes international
relations, public policy, political science, history, economics, law,
philosophy, math, and CS backgrounds.
**Fit:** governance-focused researchers wanting a structured Oxford
season.

### [IAPS — Institute for AI Policy and Strategy](https://www.iaps.ai/fellowship)

3-month fully-funded AI policy fellowship. Two-week in-person residency
in Washington DC, then independent project with expert mentor. No
technical expertise required.
**Fit:** people from policy, legal, and adjacent backgrounds who want
a real policy project and a DC network.

### [MATS — ML Alignment & Theory Scholars](https://www.matsprogram.org/)

10-week full-time research program in Berkeley and London. ~120 fellows
paired with mentors from Anthropic, UK AISI, Redwood Research, ARC, and
others. Streams: empirical alignment, interpretability, policy /
strategy, technical governance, compute. $5,000/month stipend + $8,000/
month compute. Summer and Autumn cohorts.
**Fit:** technical alignment or policy researchers ready for a focused
full-time sprint.

### [Pivotal Research Fellowship](https://www.pivotal-research.org/fellowship)

9-week in-person program at LISA, London. Q1 and Q3 cohorts. £6,000–
£8,000 stipend plus travel, housing, meals, compute. Focus: AI safety,
AIxBio, AI governance. 129 alumni; 9.1/10 peer recommendation.
**Fit:** people wanting a London-based intensive with a strong peer
cohort and AIxBio openness.

### [SPAR — Supervised Program for Alignment Research](https://sparai.org/)

Part-time (5–40 hrs/week), fully remote, 3-month program. Connects
aspiring researchers with experienced mentors on AI safety and
governance projects. Most projects result in papers; demo day with
METR, Redwood Research, GovAI, MATS.
**Fit:** people with day jobs or other constraints who want serious
mentorship without quitting.

### [Talos Network Fellowship](https://www.talosnetwork.org/talos-fellowship)

European AI-policy career launchpad. AI Policy Fundamentals training →
6-day Brussels summit → optional paid 4–6 month placement at a leading
AI governance organisation.
**Fit:** people early in their European AI policy careers; the
placement track is rare and valuable.

## How I'd start if I were you (week 1)

1. **Pick one.** Don't apply to five. Read the websites of the top
   three that match your trajectory; identify which one is actually the
   best fit; commit to it.
2. **Reach out to two prior fellows.** Ask: what did you actually do?
   What was the application bar? What would you do differently?
3. **Draft the project proposal.** A specific question, not a topic
   area. "How do current frontier-model evaluation harnesses handle
   X?" not "AI evaluations."
4. **Schedule the application.** Most have deadlines 4–8 weeks out
   from when applications open. Backwards-plan from the deadline;
   submit a week early.

## Common failure modes

- **Shotgun applications.** Five generic apps don't add up to one
  strong one. The acceptance rate on strong specific applications is
  much higher than the math implies.
- **Vague project proposals.** "I want to study AI governance" loses
  to "I want to map the agencies in the U.S. federal landscape with AI
  compliance authority and write a 6,000-word landscape doc on the
  overlaps." Specificity is signal.
- **Treating fellowships as alternatives to the menu.** Most
  fellowships expect you to ship a deliverable. The skills you build
  via the menu (writing a memo, scoping a tracker, drafting a landscape
  doc) are exactly the skills the application demonstrates.
