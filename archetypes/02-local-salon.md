# Archetype 2: Local salon / reading group

> Run a 4–6 week course for 4–6 senior practitioners in your city, on a topic
> you adapted from the BlueDot curriculum.

## Why this archetype exists

The BlueDot curriculum is global by default. Its strength is reach; its
limitation is that it can't speak to the specific institutional context of
your city, your agency, your industry. A local adaptation — a salon course
for senior practitioners who already have decision-making authority — turns
the curriculum into hyperlocal influence.

This is the move that compounds. Every person who completes your salon
brings AI-governance literacy back into a room you couldn't access. Five
participants from five different agencies is five rooms.

## When to pick this

- You're a comfortable facilitator. You don't have to be a content expert
  on the original curriculum — you have to be able to hold a room.
- You have 4–6 senior people in your adjacent network who would come if
  you asked. Test this honestly: would five of them say yes to a six-week
  commitment in the next two weeks?
- You have 6–10 hours a week for 6–10 weeks (planning + sessions + follow-ups).
- You're energized rather than drained by group dynamics.

## When NOT to pick this

- You can't yet recruit five senior people. Build that relational capital
  first via archetypes #3, #5, or #7; come back to this in six months.
- You want this to "build community" without a specific learning outcome.
  Salons that don't promise an outcome don't get senior participants.
- You'd rather write than facilitate. Pick #4 or #5 instead.

## What v1 looks like

A 4–6 session course that:

1. Has 4–6 senior participants from adjacent fields (your jurisdiction,
   different agencies/firms/departments).
2. Meets weekly for 90 minutes, in person ideally.
3. Operates under Chatham House Rules so people can speak candidly.
4. Has reading assignments per session, drawn from the BlueDot curriculum
   plus 1–2 local pieces.
5. Includes at least one tabletop exercise or facilitated dialogue rather
   than just discussion.

You're done with v1 when you've finished the final session and at least
three participants tell you, unprompted, that they want a continuation.

## What v2/v3 looks like

- A second cohort with the previous cohort as co-recruiters.
- A published playbook so others can replicate it (which is itself a
  hand-off artifact — see the example).
- An ongoing alumni network with quarterly convenings.
- A shared deliverable: the cohort co-authors a memo or a brief.

## Example in the wild

[agi-strategy-government](https://github.com/Khullani/agi-strategy-government)
— the AGI Strategy curriculum adapted for senior Chicago government
practitioners, five weeks, four participants, Chatham House Rules.

## How I'd start if I were you (week 1)

1. **List your top 12 candidates.** Senior, adjacent-field, in your city.
   You'll invite 8 to get 5.
2. **Pick the curriculum slice.** Not the whole BlueDot course — one
   coherent arc (4–6 sessions). Default to AGI Strategy or Governance.
3. **Write the invitation email.** Concrete: dates, location, time
   commitment, what they get, why you picked them personally.
4. **Pre-commit a venue and dates** before you send invitations. People
   say yes to specifics, no to "would you be interested if…"

## Common failure modes

- **Recruiting peers instead of seniors.** Peer reading groups are fine
  but lower-leverage. The whole point is access via senior practitioners.
- **Letting sessions become open discussion.** Without a structured
  exercise per session, the conversation defaults to whatever the most
  vocal person wants to talk about. Tabletop exercises fix this.
- **No follow-up after session 6.** The relationships are the artifact.
  Plan the alumni-style follow-up before you start, not after.
