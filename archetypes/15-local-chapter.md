# Archetype 15: Local chapter / reading group

> Open-membership reading group or community at your campus or city — student-led, ongoing, sustained past month 3.

## Why this archetype exists

A local chapter is how the field gets denser in your specific place.
It's not the closed senior salon of archetype #2 (4–6 senior
practitioners, structured curriculum, 4–6 weeks). It's open membership,
ongoing, often student-led, and lighter touch — a recurring reading
group, a campus AI safety society, a city-level monthly meetup, the
local node of a larger network.

This is also the archetype that does **local evangelism well** —
spreading AI safety, AI governance, or AI risk thinking to the people
in your physical proximity who'd otherwise not encounter it. Done
right, it compounds: a chapter that survives past month 3 with 8+
regulars becomes the recruiting ground for the next cohort, the next
salon, the next convening.

## When to pick this

**Best fit for:** any of the four BlueDot courses (FAIG, AGIS, BIOS, TAIS).

- You're at a **university, in a city, or in a workplace** where you
  can credibly host a recurring meeting (campus room, coffee shop with
  a back area, library, a willing employer).
- You have **4–8 hours a month** for sustained organizing (scheduling,
  recruiting, running, debriefing).
- You're **energized by building rooms** more than by writing
  artifacts.
- You can identify a **successor** — a co-organizer who'd run it if
  you stepped back. Chapter resilience is the whole game.

## When NOT to pick this

- **You'd be the only organizer with no successor.** Chapters die when
  the founding organizer burns out; pick this only if you can name the
  second person from the start.
- **You can't sustain past month 3.** The first three meetings are
  easy; meetings 4–10 are where the chapter either crystallizes or
  dies. Pick this only if you can commit to the long middle.
- **You'd be promising "community" without a structured deliverable
  per meeting.** Reading groups without an artifact per session drift;
  the structure is what keeps the room useful.
- **You'd be doing this because no senior practitioners said yes to
  archetype #2 (salon).** Different audiences, different shapes —
  chapter is not a consolation prize.

## What v1 looks like

A sustained chapter at 3 months:

1. **3+ meetings completed**, on a regular cadence (weekly, biweekly,
   or monthly).
2. **8+ regulars** — people who've come to at least 2 meetings, not
   all the same 4 friends.
3. **A named successor** — a co-organizer who'd run the next 3
   meetings if you stepped back.
4. **A per-meeting artifact** — meeting notes, a one-page summary of
   what was read and what people argued, a list of next-meeting topics.
   Saved somewhere new members can catch up by reading backward.
5. **At least one member who's launched a v1 of another archetype** as
   a result of being in the chapter.

You're done with v1 when meetings 4 and 5 are scheduled and the
successor is named.

## What to do after v1

- **Run a small convening (archetype #7)** with the most engaged
  chapter members as a deliverable: take what's been discussed and
  produce a memo, a panel, a written argument.
- **Graduate the most engaged regulars into a closed senior salon
  (#2)** when the chapter has the right people. The chapter is the
  funnel; the salon is the concentrated room.
- **Connect with other chapters** — most cities and universities now
  have AI safety / AI governance chapters. Cross-chapter coordination
  compounds.

## How I'd start if I were you (week 1)

1. **Pick the venue, day, and time.** Recurring meetings need stable
   logistics. Decide now.
2. **Identify the successor.** Ask one person to co-organize with you
   from the start. Two people sustain; one burns out.
3. **Draft the first three meeting topics.** A reading per meeting, a
   discussion question, a 15-minute structured exercise. Concrete >
   aspirational.
4. **Invite 12 people for the first meeting.** Expect 6 to show. Use
   the BlueDot / AISST / EA-uni chapter playbooks as references for
   recruitment.

## Common failure modes

- **No structure per meeting.** "Open discussion" defaults to whoever's
  most vocal. Each meeting needs a reading + a discussion question + a
  small exercise.
- **No successor.** The chapter dies the week the founding organizer
  is busy. Plan for handover from week one.
- **Evangelism without recruitment.** Talking about AI safety locally
  isn't enough; the chapter needs people who keep coming back.
  Recruitment is the work.
- **Inviting only friends.** Friends will come for two meetings as a
  favor. Members come because the chapter is useful. Build for the
  second group.
- **Skipping meeting notes.** Without a written trace, new members
  can't catch up and old members forget what was decided. Meeting #1
  needs notes posted before meeting #2.

## Combinations

- **#15 (local chapter) → #2 (local salon)** — chapter regulars who
  level up become the recruitment pool for a closed senior salon.
- **#15 (local chapter) → #7 (convening)** — the chapter's most
  engaged members produce a deliverable in a structured convening.
- **#15 (local chapter) → #8 (facilitate next cohort)** —
  chapter-running is the apprenticeship that prepares you for
  facilitating a full BlueDot cohort.
