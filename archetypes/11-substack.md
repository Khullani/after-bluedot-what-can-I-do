# Archetype 11: Substack — weekly post for four weeks

> A short-run Substack discipline: one published post per week for four weeks. The point is the thinking, not the audience.

## Why this archetype exists

Clear writing is clear thinking. After a course your head is full of
half-formed positions; the question is whether they survive contact with
a sentence you'd actually publish. Four weeks of weekly posts is enough
discipline to find out, without committing to a year of sustained
publishing.

The output is secondary. The primary output is *you* — sharper, more
able to defend a position in a meeting, more willing to take a public
stance because you've already taken one in writing.

## When to pick this

**Best fit for:** any of the four BlueDot courses (FAIG, AGIS, BIOS, TAIS).

- You finish the course with strong intuitions but can't yet defend
  them under pressure. Writing is how you find out what you actually
  think.
- You have 3–4 hours a week for the writing itself: drafting, editing,
  publishing.
- You'd benefit more from cadence than from one polished essay — short,
  frequent posts surface more inconsistencies than one long one.
- You can name three or four questions or arguments that have been
  turning over in your head since the course. Each becomes a post.

## When NOT to pick this

- You're hoping to "build an audience." That's a different commitment —
  months, not weeks — and a different shape. Use this for the thinking,
  not for the readers.
- You don't have a clear sense of *what* to write about. If you can't
  name three concrete topics, do a landscape doc (#4) or a memo (#3)
  instead.
- You'd shame-spiral if a post lands at 80% rather than 100%. The whole
  point is to publish at 80%.

## What v1 looks like

Four published posts over four weeks. Each:

1. **400–1,200 words.** Long enough to make a claim; short enough to
   finish in one sitting.
2. **One specific claim per post**, defended with whatever combination
   of argument and evidence you can muster in a Saturday morning.
3. **Published, not just drafted.** The discipline only works if it's
   public.
4. **Posted Sundays before midnight.** Cadence beats polish.

You're done with v1 when post #4 ships.

## What to do after v1

- **Notice which post surprised you to write.** That's the angle for
  the next four-week run (or for archetype #4 or #5).
- **Notice which post you most resisted writing.** That's the position
  you're least sure of — useful to know.
- **Email the post that landed best to two people who'd disagree with
  it.** Their response is more useful than the post itself.

## How I'd start if I were you (week 1)

1. **Pick the platform.** Substack default — lowest friction, native
   subscribe flow, email-first.
2. **Pick four topics.** Write the title and one sentence of claim for
   each, this Sunday. If you can't, write *one* and let the others
   emerge.
3. **Draft post #1 Saturday.** Publish Sunday before midnight. Sleep
   on edits — publish anyway.
4. **Repeat three weeks.** Don't iterate on past posts; write the next
   one.

## Common failure modes

- **Polishing forever.** Post #1 doesn't have to be the best thing
  you'll ever write. It has to be public. The next three sharpen what
  #1 didn't.
- **No specific claim.** A post that gestures at an interesting area
  is not a post. Each post needs a sentence the reader can disagree
  with.
- **Treating audience as the metric.** Twelve subscribers, kept up
  consistently, will route more in twelve months than a private notes
  file does. The point of the cadence is *you* getting sharper.
  Audience compounds later.
