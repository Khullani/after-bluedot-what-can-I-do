# Archetype 7: Convening / Panel / Workshop

> Get the right eight people in a room for ninety minutes, with a specific
> output in mind.

## Why this archetype exists

A well-designed convening is the fastest way to compress months of
relationship-building into an afternoon. It creates introductions that
would otherwise take a year of coffees, surfaces disagreements that
written-only formats hide, and — if you do the output design right —
produces something tangible that the participants can point to and that
gives them a reason to stay in touch with you afterward.

The trap is treating the convening as the goal. The convening is the
event; the output and the relationships are the goal. If you forget that,
you've thrown a party.

## When to pick this

- You can name the eight people you'd want in the room, with their real
  names, and you have a credible reason each of them would say yes.
- You have a specific output in mind: a memo, a question set, a working
  agreement, a list of next-step commitments. Not "a great conversation."
- You have 15–30 hours over 4–8 weeks (recruiting + logistics + design +
  follow-up is more work than people think).
- You enjoy hosting and won't resent the operational work.

## When NOT to pick this

- You're hoping a convening will reveal what the right topic is. The
  topic and the output have to be locked before the invitation goes
  out, or senior people won't come.
- You can't name the eight people. "I'd invite some folks from the
  governance community" is not a guest list, it's an aspiration.
- You don't have a venue or a budget. Coffee shops are not venues for
  90-minute structured conversations with senior people.

## What v1 looks like

A single 60–120 minute session that:

1. Has 6–10 invited participants, named in advance, who confirmed.
2. Has a clear pre-read circulated 5–7 days ahead.
3. Has a written agenda — not just a topic — with structured time blocks.
4. Produces a concrete artifact by the end of the session: a memo, a
   question set, a Chatham House summary, a list of commitments.
5. Has a follow-up email to all participants within 48 hours sharing
   the output and the next step.

You're done with v1 when the artifact is written and circulated, and
at least two participants reply that they'd attend the next one.

## What v2/v3 looks like

- A recurring quarterly convening with a stable core group.
- A published artifact series — each convening produces a public memo
  that links to the prior ones.
- A workshop format at a larger conference, using the lessons from your
  closed convenings as content.

## Example in the wild

[`examples/07-convening.md`](../examples/07-convening.md) — **AI Safety
Chicago** ([luma.com/ais-chicago](https://luma.com/ais-chicago)),
co-hosted by Techne AI and BlueDot Impact. First of a recurring series.
~120 attendees from Chicago universities and regulatory/legal employers,
each event producing a white paper. The named output (the white paper)
is what separates this from a meetup; the recurring format is what lets
the outputs compound.

combination** named in

External convening traditions worth studying: Chatham House and Carnegie
— what they do at scale you can do for 8 people in your city. Local
shapes: a 90-minute working session with state legislators' staff before
a vote; a workshop with 3 reporters and 3 researchers before a major
paper drops.

## How I'd start if I were you (week 1)

1. **Lock the topic and the output in one sentence.** "At the end of
   this convening, we will have produced X." If you can't fill in X,
   you're not ready.
2. **List the 8 named invitees.** Confirm with 1–2 of them informally
   that the topic resonates before you send the formal invite.
3. **Pick the date and the venue.** A real room, with a door that
   closes. Decide whether it's Chatham House (default yes for senior
   participants).
4. **Draft the invitation.** Specific date, time, who else is invited,
   pre-read, what they're committing to.

## Common failure modes

- **No output design.** "Let's have a great conversation" produces
  conversations that fade by Monday. Pick the output before you pick
  the date.
- **Inviting too many people.** Eight is plenty. Above twelve, people
  stop talking and start performing.
- **Skipping the pre-read.** Without it, the first 30 minutes is
  context-setting and you get one substantive hour.
- **No follow-up artifact.** If nothing leaves the room, nothing
  compounds.
